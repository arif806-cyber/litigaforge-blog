# UK Indefinite Leave to Remain 2026: 5‑Year Route Requirements & Checklist

Wondering how to secure Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) in the UK in 2026 via the 5‑year route? This guide breaks down every legal requirement and provides a practical checklist to help you apply with confidence.

## Eligibility Criteria under the Immigration Rules Part 9

The UK’s Immigration Rules, specifically Part 9 – Settlement, set out the statutory framework for Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR). To qualify under the 5‑year route in 2026, an applicant must have lawfully resided in the UK for a continuous period of five years on a qualifying visa category, such as a Tier 2 (General) work visa, Tier 4 (Student) visa leading to a Skilled Worker route, or a Family visa under Appendix FM. Continuous residence means no more than 180 days of absence in any 12‑month period, as stipulated in Paragraph 322(2) of the Immigration Rules. Applicants must also meet the English language requirement under Appendix FM (Section 2), which can be satisfied by passing an approved English test at CEFR level B1 or holding a degree taught in English. The Life in the UK Test (Form SETA 6) is mandatory, and the applicant must demonstrate sufficient knowledge of British customs, history and government. Financial requirements differ by visa type; for example, Skilled Worker applicants must show a minimum salary of £26,200 (or the ‘going rate’ for the occupation) as per the Immigration (Salary) Guidance 2025. Criminality checks are performed under the Immigration Act 1971 Schedule 2, requiring disclosure of any convictions. A conviction under the UK’s Terrorism Act 2000 (Section 5) or a sentence of 12 months or more will lead to automatic refusal. The Home Office also assesses compliance with the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) and ensures the applicant has paid the full amount for the 5‑year period. Understanding these statutory thresholds is essential before proceeding to the application stage.

> **Key takeaway:** Confirm you have five years continuous lawful residence, meet English and Life in the UK test requirements, and satisfy any salary or financial thresholds.

## Document Checklist: Core Evidence Required for ILR

A successful ILR application hinges on submitting a comprehensive documentary package. Core documents include: (1) A valid passport covering the entire 5‑year residence period, with all UK entry/exit stamps; (2) Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) or residence card for each visa held; (3) Proof of continuous residence – utility bills, council tax statements, or bank statements dated at least monthly for five years; (4) Employment evidence – P60s, payslips covering the last 12 months, and a letter from your employer confirming salary, job title and start date, referencing the Skilled Worker Salary Threshold (Immigration Rules Part 6, Schedule 8); (5) English language certification – IELTS for UKVI (overall band ≥5.5) or an academic degree certificate with a letter confirming instruction was in English; (6) Life in the UK Test pass certificate (Form SETA 6); (7) Criminal record check – a recent Police Certificate (if required) and disclosure of any convictions under the Immigration Act 1971 Schedule 2; (8) Evidence of payment of the Immigration Health Surcharge – receipt from NHS Business Services Authority; (9) Proof of relationship (for family routes) – marriage certificate, joint tenancy agreement, or birth certificates of children. Each document must be an original or a certified copy, and translations are required for any non‑English documents, accompanied by a certified translation. The Home Office’s Appendix V provides detailed guidance on acceptable evidence, and failure to include any item may result in a ‘Refusal on the grounds of insufficient evidence’ under Paragraph 322(1)(a). Applicants should also retain a copy of the completed SET(O) form and the payment receipt for the £2,389 (as of 2026) application fee.

> **Key takeaway:** Gather original passports, BRPs, continuous residence proof, employment records, English test results, Life in the UK certificate, and all fee receipts before starting the online form.

## Step‑by‑Step Application Process on the UK Visas & Immigration Portal

The UK Visas & Immigration (UKVI) portal streamlines the ILR application into four distinct stages. Stage 1 – Create an online account on the gov.uk website and select ‘Apply for Indefinite Leave to Remain’. Upload the SET(O) form, which replaces the old SET(O) paper form, and answer the eligibility questionnaire that cross‑checks your visa history against Paragraph 322(1). Stage 2 – Pay the application fee (£2,389 for adults in 2026) and the Immigration Health Surcharge (£624 per year). Payment is processed via secure card transaction; a receipt is generated automatically. Stage 3 – Book a biometric appointment at a UKVI Visa Application Centre (VAC). You must provide fingerprints and a digital photograph; the VAC will issue a receipt confirming biometric collection, which must be uploaded to your portal profile. Stage 4 – Submit the supporting documents. The portal accepts PDF files up to 10 MB each; ensure each document is clearly legible and named according to the UKVI’s naming convention (e.g., ‘Passport_Page1.pdf’). After submission, the system generates a reference number; you can track progress via the ‘View Application Status’ feature. The Home Office aims to decide within 8 weeks for standard routes, but complex cases may take up to 12 weeks under Paragraph 322(6). If additional information is required, a ‘Request for Further Information’ (RFI) will be issued, and you must respond within 14 days to avoid refusal. Upon approval, you will receive a decision letter and a new BRP confirming ILR status, which is valid for 10 years and renewable.

> **Key takeaway:** Complete the online SET(O) form, pay fees, attend a biometric appointment, upload all documents, and monitor the portal for decisions or RFIs.

## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Refusal under Paragraph 322

Refusal under Paragraph 322 of the Immigration Rules often stems from preventable errors. The most frequent issues include: (i) Gaps in continuous residence – even a single trip exceeding 180 days in a 12‑month window triggers a breach; mitigate by keeping detailed travel logs and providing supporting airline itineraries. (ii) Inadequate salary evidence – employers sometimes issue generic letters lacking the required ‘going rate’ reference; request a detailed letter that cites the exact salary figure and the occupation’s Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) code. (iii) Insufficient English proof – an IELTS result below the required band or a degree without an official English language statement leads to automatic failure; retake the test or obtain a ‘Letter of Confirmation of English Language Medium’ from your university. (iv) Missing or illegible documents – scanned copies that are blurry or cropped are rejected; use a high‑resolution scanner and verify each page before upload. (v) Failure to disclose prior immigration violations – any previous refusal, overstays, or curfew breaches must be declared; omission is considered deception under the Immigration Act 1971 Section 10 and results in a mandatory refusal. To safeguard against these pitfalls, conduct a pre‑submission audit using the UKVI’s ‘Checklist for ILR’ (Appendix V), and consider engaging a qualified immigration solicitor for a document review. The Home Office also offers a ‘Pre‑Check Service’ for a modest fee, providing feedback on completeness before formal submission.

> **Key takeaway:** Avoid refusal by ensuring no residence gaps, providing precise salary letters, meeting English standards, uploading clear documents, and fully disclosing any past immigration issues.

## Post‑Approval: Rights, Responsibilities and Path to British Citizenship

Once ILR is granted, the holder enjoys the same rights as a permanent resident under the Immigration Act 1971 Section 3, including the ability to work without restriction, access public funds, and reside indefinitely. However, ILR is not equivalent to citizenship; it can be revoked if the holder spends more than 2 consecutive years outside the UK, as per Paragraph 322(4). ILR holders must also continue to meet the public good test under the Nationality, Immigration and Asylum Act 2002 Schedule 2, which assesses any conduct that might threaten public safety. For those aiming for British citizenship, the naturalisation route requires a further 12 months of residence after ILR (or 5 years for spouses of British citizens) and compliance with the ‘Good Character’ requirement, which includes no recent convictions under the Criminal Justice Act 2003 Section 145. Applicants must submit Form AN, pass another Life in the UK Test (if not taken within the last 2 years), and demonstrate ongoing English proficiency. The fee for naturalisation in 2026 is £1,330, and the decision timeframe is typically 6 months. Holding ILR also enables the holder to apply for a UK passport, travel visa‑free to over 180 countries, and sponsor family members under the Family Migration Rules (Appendix FM). Maintaining up‑to‑date records, such as renewing the BRP before its expiry, ensures uninterrupted rights.

> **Key takeaway:** After ILR, enjoy permanent residency rights, avoid long absences, and plan a 12‑month stay before applying for British citizenship.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the minimum continuous residence period for the 5‑year ILR route?

You must have lived in the UK lawfully for five consecutive years with no single absence exceeding 180 days in any 12‑month period.

### Do I need to retake the Life in the UK Test after getting ILR?

No, the test result is valid for life; you only need to retake it if applying for naturalisation and your original test was taken more than two years ago.

### Can I apply for ILR while on a Tier 2 (General) visa that is about to expire?

Yes, you can submit the ILR application up to 28 days before your current visa expires, provided you meet all other eligibility criteria.

### How long does the Home Office usually take to decide on an ILR application?

Standard decisions are made within 8 weeks; complex cases may take up to 12 weeks under Paragraph 322(6) of the Immigration Rules.
