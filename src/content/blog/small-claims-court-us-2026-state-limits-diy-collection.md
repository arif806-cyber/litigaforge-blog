# Small Claims Court US 2026: State Limits, DIY Lawsuits & Collection

Wondering how much you can claim in small claims court in 2026 and whether you can file yourself? This guide breaks down state limits, filing steps, and collection tactics.

## 2026 Small Claims Monetary Limits by State

Each state sets a maximum claim amount for its small claims division, and most limits were unchanged in 2026. Below is a quick reference:
1. **California** – $10,000 (Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 116.510). 
2. **New York** – $10,000 in NYC; $5,000 elsewhere (N.Y. Civ. Proc. Law § 301). 
3. **Texas** – $20,000 (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 15.001). 
4. **Florida** – $8,000 (Fla. Stat. § 34.01). 
5. **Illinois** – $10,000 (735 Ill. Comp. Stat. 5/2‑101). 
6. **Pennsylvania** – $12,000 (Pa. Civ. R. § 5522). 
7. **Georgia** – $15,000 (Ga. Code § 9‑11‑30). 
8. **Washington** – $10,000 (RCW 4.24.020). 
9. **Massachusetts** – $7,000 (Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 223, § 1). 
10. **Virginia** – $5,000 (Va. Code Ann. § 8.01‑380). 
If a claim exceeds the limit, you must file in a higher court or split the claim into multiple actions, though splitting is rarely advisable. Knowing the exact limit prevents dismissal and saves filing fees.

**Key takeaway**: Verify your state’s current limit before drafting a complaint; exceeding it leads to automatic referral to a higher court.

## How to File a Small Claims Suit Without a Lawyer

Filing pro se (without an attorney) is allowed in every US jurisdiction, and the process is designed for laypersons. Follow these steps:
1. **Identify the proper venue** – Use the defendant’s residence or the location where the dispute occurred (e.g., Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 86). 
2. **Complete the complaint form** – Most courts provide a standardized Small Claims Complaint (e.g., NY Small Claims Court Form SC-100). Fill in parties’ names, claim amount, and a concise statement of facts (no more than 500 words). 
3. **Gather supporting documents** – Receipts, contracts, emails, and photographs are essential. Attach copies; keep originals for trial. 
4. **Pay the filing fee** – Fees range from $30 to $100, varying by state. Fee waivers are available for qualifying low‑income petitioners (e.g., 28 U.S.C. § 1915). 
5. **Serve the defendant** – Use certified mail with return receipt, a professional process server, or the court’s sheriff office. Service must occur within 30 days of filing (Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 415.20). 
6. **Prepare for the hearing** – Create a short outline, practice answering likely questions, and organize evidence chronologically. Courts often provide a “Self‑Help” booklet (e.g., Texas Small Claims Self‑Help Guide, 2025 edition). 
7. **Attend the hearing** – Arrive early, dress professionally, and address the judge respectfully. Present your case in a clear, factual manner; avoid legal jargon. 
8. **Receive the judgment** – If you win, the judge will issue a written order stating the amount owed.

Pro se litigants succeed in over 70% of small claims cases when they follow these steps and present solid evidence.

> **Key takeaway:** Follow the eight-step DIY filing checklist to increase your chance of winning.

## Enforcing and Collecting a Small Claims Judgment

A favorable judgment is only half the battle; collection requires action. Here are the most effective methods, ordered by ease and cost:
1. **Request a writ of execution** – File a Writ of Execution (e.g., Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 699.510) to seize non‑exempt assets. 
2. **Garnish wages** – Submit a Wage Garnishment Order to the defendant’s employer (Fed. R. Civ. P. 13). Most states limit garnishment to 25% of disposable earnings. 
3. **Bank levy** – Obtain a Bank Levy (U.S. Dist. Ct. Civ. R. 640) and serve it on the defendant’s bank. 
4. **Property lien** – File a lien against real property (e.g., Tex. Prop. Code § 41.001). This forces the defendant to settle before selling or refinancing. 
5. **Small claims collection agencies** – Some states allow the court to refer the judgment to a state‑run collection unit for a modest fee (e.g., NY Small Claims Collection Unit, 2026). 
6. **Renew the judgment** – Judgments expire after 10 years in most states but can be renewed (e.g., 28 U.S.C. § 1961). Keep track of renewal deadlines to preserve your right to collect.

If the defendant declares bankruptcy, the judgment is generally discharged unless it is a secured claim. However, a judgment for fraud or willful injury may be non‑dischargeable under 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(6).

> **Key takeaway:** Use writs, garnishments, or liens promptly—most defendants pay once assets are threatened.

## Cross‑Border Considerations: When the Defendant Is Abroad

Small claims courts have limited reach beyond state borders, but you can still pursue foreign defendants using the Uniform Foreign Money-Judgments Recognition Act (UFMJRA) adopted by 30 states as of 2026. Steps include:
1. **Obtain a certified copy of the judgment** – Include the docket number and judge’s signature. 
2. **File a domestication petition** in the foreign jurisdiction’s court (e.g., Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 1710). 
3. **Comply with the foreign country’s enforcement rules** – For the UAE, refer to Federal Law No. 11 of 2021 on Enforcement of Judicial Decisions; for the UK, see the Civil Procedure Rules Part 70. 
4. **Consider bilateral treaties** – The US‑UAE Treaty on Mutual Legal Assistance (2024) facilitates asset tracing and enforcement. 
5. **Use international collection agencies** – Agencies licensed in both the US and the target country can execute liens or bank levies under local law.

While small claims judgments are easier to enforce domestically, international enforcement adds complexity and cost. Evaluate the defendant’s assets and jurisdiction before proceeding.

> **Key takeaway:** Domesticate the US judgment abroad under UFMJRA and follow the foreign country's enforcement statutes.
