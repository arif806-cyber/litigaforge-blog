# India Patent Filing 2026

As an Indian startup, navigating the patent filing process can be daunting, with key decisions to make on provisional vs complete specification and associated costs. Understanding the Indian patent filing process, as per the Patents Act 1970, is crucial for startups to protect their intellectual property and avoid potential pitfalls.

## Introduction to Patent Filing in India

The Patents Act 1970 and the Patents Rules 2003 govern the patent filing process in India. As per Section 6 of the Patents Act, a patent application can be filed with the Indian Patent Office, which can be either a provisional application or a complete application. Startups must consider the benefits and drawbacks of each type of application, including the costs and timelines involved. For instance, a provisional application can be filed with a lower fee, as per the Patents Rules 2003, and provides a 12-month window to file a complete application. However, if the complete application is not filed within the stipulated timeframe, the provisional application will be deemed abandoned, as per Section 9 of the Patents Act.

> **Key takeaway:** Indian startups must carefully consider the type of patent application to file, taking into account the costs, timelines, and potential risks involved.

## Provisional Patent Specification

A provisional patent specification, as per Section 10 of the Patents Rules 2003, is a temporary application that allows startups to file for a patent without having to provide a detailed description of the invention. This type of application is often used when the invention is still in the development stage, and the startup wants to secure a priority date for the invention. The provisional application must include a description of the invention, drawings, and claims, as per Form 1 of the Patents Rules 2003. The fee for filing a provisional application is currently ₹2,600 for individuals and ₹13,000 for companies, as per the Patents Rules 2003. Startups must ensure that the provisional application is complete and accurate, as it will form the basis for the complete application.

> **Key takeaway:** Startups must ensure that the provisional patent specification is complete and accurate, as it will form the basis for the complete application.

## Complete Patent Specification

A complete patent specification, as per Section 10 of the Patents Rules 2003, is a detailed application that includes a full description of the invention, drawings, and claims. This type of application is often used when the invention is fully developed, and the startup wants to secure a patent for the invention. The complete application must include a detailed description of the invention, including its technical features and advantages, as per Form 2 of the Patents Rules 2003. The fee for filing a complete application is currently ₹4,600 for individuals and ₹26,000 for companies, as per the Patents Rules 2003. Startups must ensure that the complete application is thorough and accurate, as it will be examined by the Indian Patent Office to determine whether the invention is patentable.

> **Key takeaway:** Startups must ensure that the complete patent specification is thorough and accurate, as it will be examined by the Indian Patent Office to determine whether the invention is patentable.

The cost of patent filing in India varies depending on the type of application and the fees associated with it.
