# RTI India 2026

Filing a Right to Information (RTI) application in India can be a daunting task, but with the right guidance, citizens can exercise their fundamental right to information under the RTI Act 2005. In this comprehensive guide, we will walk you through the step-by-step process of filing an RTI application in India, covering the essential laws, timelines, and practical steps involved.

## Introduction to RTI Act 2005

The RTI Act 2005 is a landmark legislation that empowers Indian citizens to seek information from public authorities, promoting transparency and accountability in governance. Under Section 2(f) of the RTI Act, 'information' includes any material in any form, including records, documents, memos, e-mails, opinions, advice, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, reports, papers, samples, models, data material held in any electronic form. To file an RTI application, citizens must first identify the public authority they wish to seek information from, such as government departments, public sector undertakings, or local bodies. The RTI Act also prescribes a fee of Rs. 10 for filing an application, which can be paid through demand draft, banker's cheque, or cash, as per Section 6 of the Act.

> **Key takeaway:** Citizens can file an RTI application to seek information from public authorities, promoting transparency and accountability in governance.

## Eligibility and Requirements

Any citizen of India can file an RTI application, as per Section 3 of the RTI Act 2005. However, the applicant must provide their name, address, and contact details, as well as a clear and specific description of the information sought. The application must be in writing, either in English or the official language of the state, and must be accompanied by the prescribed fee. The applicant can also seek information in the form of certified copies of documents, as per Section 7(1) of the Act. Additionally, the RTI Act also provides for exemptions from disclosure of certain information, such as those related to national security, as per Section 8(1)(a) of the Act.

> **Key takeaway:** Citizens must provide their name, address, and contact details, as well as a clear description of the information sought, to file a valid RTI application.

## Procedure for Filing RTI Application

The procedure for filing an RTI application involves several steps: (1) identify the public authority, (2) prepare the application, (3) pay the fee, (4) submit the application, and (5) receive the response. The applicant must submit the application to the Central Public Information Officer (CPIO) or State Public Information Officer (SPIO) of the concerned public authority, as per Section 5(1) of the Act. The CPIO/SPIO must provide a response within 30 days of receipt of the application, as per Section 7(1) of the Act. In case of delays or denial of information, the applicant can file an appeal to the Appellate Authority, as per Section 19(1) of the Act.

> **Key takeaway:** Citizens must submit their RTI application to the CPIO/SPIO of the concerned public authority and receive a response within 30 days.

The RTI Act provides for certain exemptions from disclosure of information, such as those related to national security, as per Section 8(1)(a) of the Act. However, these exemptions are subject to the public interest test, as per Section 8(2) of the Act.
