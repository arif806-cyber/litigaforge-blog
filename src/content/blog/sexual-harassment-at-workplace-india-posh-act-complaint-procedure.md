# India 2026: POSH Act

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (POSH Act) is a crucial legislation in India that aims to prevent and address sexual harassment at the workplace. In this article, we will outline the step-by-step procedure for filing a POSH Act complaint in India, providing a comprehensive guide for employees and employers alike.

## Understanding the POSH Act

The POSH Act, which came into effect on December 9, 2013, defines sexual harassment as any unwelcome act or behavior, whether directly or by implication, including physical contact, advances, or requests for sexual favors. According to Section 3 of the POSH Act, every employer is required to constitute an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) to investigate and redress complaints of sexual harassment. The ICC must consist of a minimum of four members, including a presiding officer, two members from the workplace, and one member from a non-governmental organization. The POSH Act also stipulates that the ICC must complete its inquiry within 90 days of receiving the complaint, as per Section 11 of the Act. Employers who fail to comply with the provisions of the POSH Act can face penalties, including fines and imprisonment, under Section 26 of the Act.

> **Key takeaway:** Employers must constitute an Internal Complaints Committee to investigate and redress complaints of sexual harassment.

## Step-by-Step Complaint Procedure

To file a complaint under the POSH Act, the aggrieved employee must submit a written complaint to the ICC or the employer, as per Section 9 of the Act. The complaint must include the following details: the date, time, and location of the incident; a description of the incident; and any witnesses. The ICC will then conduct an inquiry, which may include interviewing the complainant, the respondent, and any witnesses. The ICC must provide the respondent with a copy of the complaint and give them an opportunity to respond, as per Section 10 of the Act. The ICC will then submit its report to the employer, who must take action on the recommendations, as per Section 13 of the Act.

> **Key takeaway:** The aggrieved employee must submit a written complaint to the ICC or the employer, including details of the incident and any witnesses.

## Timeline for Filing a Complaint

The POSH Act stipulates that a complaint must be filed within three months of the incident, as per Section 9 of the Act. However, the ICC may extend this time limit by a further three months, if it is satisfied that the delay was due to circumstances beyond the control of the complainant. The ICC must complete its inquiry within 90 days of receiving the complaint, as per Section 11 of the Act. If the ICC finds that the respondent has committed an act of sexual harassment, it may recommend penalties, including dismissal, as per Section 12 of the Act.

> **Key takeaway:** A complaint must be filed within three months of the incident, and the ICC must complete its inquiry within 90 days of receiving the complaint.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Employers who fail to comply with the provisions of the POSH Act can face penalties, including fines and imprisonment, under Section 26 of the Act. The penalty for non-compliance can range from Rs. 50,000 to Rs. 1,50,000, as per Section 26 of the Act. Additionally, the employer may be required to pay compensation to the aggrieved employee, as per Section 15 of the Act. The POSH Act also stipulates that the employer must take action against the respondent, including disciplinary action, as per Section 12 of the Act.
