# India 2026: Society Maintenance Disputes

If you're facing a dispute over society maintenance charges in India, it's essential to know which authority to approach for a resolution. The Consumer Protection Act 2019 and the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act 1960 are two key laws that govern such disputes in India.

## Understanding Society Maintenance Charges

Society maintenance charges are fees paid by residents to the housing society for the upkeep and maintenance of common areas. The Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act 1960, Section 79, states that every member of the society must pay their share of the expenses incurred by the society. The charges typically include expenses for amenities like security, water supply, and electricity. If you're disputing the charges, you should first review your society's bylaws and the agreement you signed when you purchased your property. The bylaws should outline the procedure for calculating and paying maintenance charges. Under the Consumer Protection Act 2019, Section 2(7), a consumer is defined as any person who purchases goods or services for a consideration. Therefore, as a resident paying society maintenance charges, you are considered a consumer and are protected under this law.

> **Key takeaway:** Review your society's bylaws and the agreement you signed to understand your obligations regarding maintenance charges.

## Authorities for Filing Complaints

If you're facing a dispute over society maintenance charges, you can approach the following authorities for a resolution: 1. Consumer Forum: You can file a complaint with the Consumer Forum under the Consumer Protection Act 2019. The forum has the power to hear complaints related to defective goods or services, including disputes over maintenance charges. 2. Co-operative Department: The Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act 1960 establishes the Co-operative Department, which regulates and supervises co-operative societies in the state. You can file a complaint with the department if you believe your society is not following the provisions of the act. 3. Registrar of Co-operative Societies: The Registrar of Co-operative Societies is responsible for registering and regulating co-operative societies. You can file a complaint with the registrar if you believe your society is not complying with the provisions of the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act 1960.

> **Key takeaway:** You can approach the Consumer Forum, Co-operative Department, or Registrar of Co-operative Societies to resolve disputes over society maintenance charges.
