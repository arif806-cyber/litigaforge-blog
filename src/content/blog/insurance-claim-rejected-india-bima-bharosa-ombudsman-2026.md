# Insurance Claim Rejected in India 2026: IRDAI Bima Bharosa & Ombudsman Remedies

Your insurance claim was denied and you’re unsure of your next move. This guide explains exactly how the Bima Bharosa Scheme and the Insurance Ombudsman can help you get relief.

## Understanding Why an Insurer Can Reject a Claim

Under the Insurance Act, 1938, Section 45, insurers may deny a claim only if the policy terms are not met, the claim is fraudulent, or the loss is not covered. The IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests) Regulations, 2020, Regulation 5(1) requires insurers to communicate the reasons for rejection in writing within 30 days of receiving the claim. Failure to do so violates Section 21 of the Bima Bharosa Scheme (2023) which mandates timely and transparent communication. For policyholders, the first step is to obtain the denial letter, verify the cited clause, and check whether the insurer complied with the statutory timelines. If the insurer ignored the 30‑day rule or gave an ambiguous reason, the denial may be deemed arbitrary, opening the door for remedial action under both the Bima Bharosa Scheme and the Insurance Ombudsman.

> **Key takeaway:** Check the denial letter for statutory compliance; any breach is a ground to challenge the rejection.

## Bima Bharosa Scheme: Immediate Relief Options

The Bima Bharosa Scheme, introduced by the IRDAI in 2023, offers a fast‑track grievance mechanism for policyholders whose claims are rejected. Section 12 of the Scheme requires insurers to deposit a "grievance fund" of 0.5% of their net premium income, which the Ombudsman can order to be paid as interim relief. To invoke the Scheme, file a written complaint with the insurer’s grievance redressal officer within 15 days of the denial (Section 13). If the insurer does not resolve the issue within 30 days, the policyholder may approach the IRDAI’s Consumer Grievance Portal (CGP) under Section 14. The CGP forwards the complaint to the Insurance Ombudsman, who must issue a provisional order within 45 days. This provisional order can include a payment of up to 50% of the claimed amount as interim relief, ensuring the policyholder’s immediate financial needs are met while the dispute is adjudicated.

> **Key takeaway:** Activate the Bima Bharosa grievance process within 15 days to secure interim relief.

## Filing a Complaint with the Insurance Ombudsman

The Insurance Ombudsman, established under the Insurance Ombudsman Act, 2020, provides a quasi‑judicial forum for claim disputes. Section 3 of the Act defines the Ombudsman's jurisdiction: any policyholder residing in India can file a complaint if the insurer has not responded within the statutory period or the response is unsatisfactory. The complaint must include the policy document, claim form, denial letter, and any supporting evidence (Section 4). Once filed, the Ombudsman issues a notice to the insurer and mandates a response within 21 days (Section 5). The Ombudsman then conducts a hearing, often via video conference, and can order the insurer to pay the claim, interest at 12% per annum (Section 7), and a penalty of up to 2% of the claim amount for non‑compliance (Section 8). The decision is binding unless appealed to the High Court within 30 days, but most insurers comply to avoid reputational damage.

> **Key takeaway:** Submit a complete complaint to the Insurance Ombudsman within 30 days of denial to trigger a binding resolution.

## Criminal Remedies under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)

If the insurer’s denial is based on fraudulent grounds—such as fabricating a non‑existent exclusion or forging documents—the policyholder can invoke criminal provisions. Section 420 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) (formerly IPC Section 420) criminalizes cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property, which includes wrongful denial of a legitimate claim. Additionally, Section 467 BNS (formerly IPC Section 467) addresses forgery of valuable security, applicable when insurers alter policy terms to evade payment. A First Information Report (FIR) can be lodged with the local police, citing the specific BNS sections and attaching the original policy, claim, and denial correspondence. The investigation may lead to prosecution, and if convicted, the insurer faces imprisonment up to three years and a fine up to Rs. 5 lakh, besides civil liability for the claim amount.

> **Key takeaway:** When fraud is evident, file an FIR invoking BNS Sections 420 and 467 to pursue criminal liability.

Draft a formal appeal referencing IRDAI Regulation 5(1) and Bima Bharosa Scheme Section 13, demanding a written response within 15 days. 4. If no satisfactory response, lodge a grievance on the IRDAI CGP (link: https://cgpr.irda.gov.in) and obtain the complaint reference number. 5. Simultaneously file a complaint with the nearest Insurance Ombudsman (state‑wise list on IRDAI website) attaching the CGP reference. 6. Keep a log of all communications, dates, and names of officials spoken to. 7. If fraud is suspected, approach the local police with an FIR citing BNS Sections 420 & 467. 8. Consider engaging a legal professional to draft a legal notice under the Insurance Act, 1938 Section 45A, which often prompts settlement before formal adjudication. Following these steps maximizes the chance of claim recovery while preserving evidence for any potential civil or criminal proceedings.

> **Key takeaway:** Systematically document and follow statutory timelines to build a compelling appeal and avoid procedural dismissals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does the Insurance Ombudsman have to decide my case?

The Ombudsman must issue a provisional order within 45 days of receiving the complaint and a final order within 90 days, if possible.

### Can I claim interest on a delayed insurance payout?

Yes, under the Insurance Ombudsman Act, 2020 Section 7, interest is payable at 12% per annum from the date of claim submission.

### What if the insurer does not have a grievance fund under Bima Bharosa?

All licensed insurers are required by IRDAI to maintain the fund; non‑compliance itself is a violation and can be reported to IRDAI for penalty.

### Is filing a criminal case necessary for every claim rejection?

No. Criminal proceedings are appropriate only when there is clear evidence of fraud or forgery, as covered under BNS Sections 420 and 467.
