## Understanding Wrongful Termination in India

Wrongful termination, also known as unfair dismissal, occurs when an employer terminates an employee's contract without a valid reason or without following the due process. In India, the Industrial Disputes Act 1947 Section 25F provides protection to employees against wrongful termination. According to this section, an employer must provide three months' notice or pay in lieu of notice before terminating an employee's contract. If the employer fails to do so, the employee can claim wrongful termination. The Indian Contract Act 1872 Section 73 also provides a remedy for breach of contract, which can be applicable in cases of wrongful termination. To establish a case for wrongful termination, an employee must prove that the termination was unfair, unreasonable, or against the principles of natural justice. The employee must also show that they have suffered financial losses or damages due to the termination. The Supreme Court of India in the case of Bharat Heavy Electricals Ltd. vs. S. Sankaranarayanan (2007) held that an employer must provide a valid reason for termination and follow the due process to avoid claims of wrongful termination.

> **Key takeaway:** Employees who have been wrongfully terminated in India can seek justice by filing a complaint under the Industrial Disputes Act 1947 or by claiming damages under the Indian Contract Act 1872.
