# UK CCJ 2026: Credit Impact

Receiving a County Court Judgement (CCJ) in the UK can significantly impact your credit score, but there are steps you can take to mitigate the damage and potentially get it set aside. Understanding the process and your rights under the UK's Civil Procedure Rules 1998 (CPR) and the County Courts Act 1984 is crucial to navigating this complex issue.

## What is a County Court Judgement (CCJ)?

A County Court Judgement is a court order issued in the UK when an individual or business fails to repay a debt. The process begins with a claimant issuing a claim form under Part 7 of the Civil Procedure Rules 1998 (CPR), which must be served on the defendant. If the defendant fails to respond or pay the debt, the claimant can apply for a judgement by default under CPR 12. This judgement can then be registered with the Register of Judgements, Orders and Fines, affecting the defendant's credit score. The Limitation Act 1980 also plays a role, as it sets out time limits for bringing claims, which can be a defence in some cases.

> **Key takeaway:** Always respond to a claim form to avoid a default judgement.

## How Does a CCJ Affect Credit?

A CCJ can severely impact an individual's or business's credit score, making it difficult to secure loans, credit cards, or mortgages in the future. Credit reference agencies like Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion will record the CCJ on the individual's credit file for six years from the date of the judgement, in accordance with the Credit Reporting Act. The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) regulates credit reference agencies, ensuring they comply with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018 when handling personal data.

> **Key takeaway:** Paying off a CCJ within a month can prevent it from being recorded on your credit file.

## Steps to Get a CCJ Set Aside

If a CCJ was issued unfairly or by mistake, it's possible to apply to have it set aside under CPR 13. The application must be made promptly, preferably within a few days of discovering the judgement, and the court will consider whether the defendant was properly served with the claim form and whether there is a valid defence to the claim. The defendant must also file an acknowledgement of service (N9 form) and a defence (N1 form) as part of the process. It's advisable to seek legal advice to ensure the application is made correctly and that all necessary evidence is provided.

> **Key takeaway:** Act quickly when applying to set aside a CCJ to avoid further action.

## Defending a Claim and Avoiding a CCJ

Defending a claim involves responding to the claim form and filing a defence. The defence should clearly outline why the claim is disputed, including any relevant evidence or witness statements. The court may direct parties to mediation under the CPR, which can be an effective way to resolve disputes without a full trial. The Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims, introduced under the Civil Procedure (Amendment No. 3) Rules 2014, also encourages parties to attempt to resolve disputes before issuing a claim. Understanding the Consumer Rights Act 2015 and the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 can also provide a basis for a defence in some cases.

> **Key takeaway:** Always seek legal advice to ensure the defence is robust and compliant with CPR.

## Paying Off a CCJ and Its Impact on Credit

Paying off a CCJ can improve an individual's credit score over time. If the debt is paid within a month of the judgement, the CCJ can be avoided from being registered on the credit file. Otherwise, once the debt is paid, the CCJ will be marked as 'satisfied' on the credit file, which can still impact credit scores but less severely than an unsatisfied CCJ. The Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974 does not apply to CCJs, but paying off the debt demonstrates responsibility and can improve future credit prospects. It's also important to check the credit file for any errors or inaccuracies, as these can be disputed under the Data Protection Act 2018.

> **Key takeaway:** Paying off a CCJ promptly can minimize its impact on credit scores.
