# UK Cohabitation 2026

As the number of unmarried couples in the UK continues to rise, it's essential to understand how to protect their assets in the event of a separation. A cohabitation agreement, also known as a living together agreement, can provide unmarried couples with legal protection and clarity on their rights and responsibilities.

## What is a Cohabitation Agreement?

A cohabitation agreement is a legally binding contract between two unmarried individuals who live together, outlining their rights and responsibilities towards each other and their shared assets. Under the UK's Family Law Act 1996, Section 63, couples can enter into a cohabitation agreement to define their financial arrangements and property ownership. This agreement can include provisions for property, finances, and childcare, providing a clear understanding of each partner's obligations and entitlements. For instance, the agreement can specify how the couple's property, such as their home, will be divided in the event of a separation. It's essential to note that cohabitation agreements are not automatically enforceable, but they can be used as evidence in court to support a couple's claims. In the case of Jones v Kernott (2011), the UK Supreme Court ruled that a cohabitation agreement can be used to determine the ownership of a property, highlighting the importance of having a clear and comprehensive agreement in place.
