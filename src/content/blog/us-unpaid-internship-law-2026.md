As the US job market continues to evolve, unpaid internships have become a topic of debate, with many wondering about their legality in 2026. ## Understanding the DOL Seven-Factor Test

The DOL seven-factor test is derived from the Supreme Court's decision in Walling v. Portland Terminal Co. (1947), which laid out the criteria for determining whether an intern is considered an employee under the FLSA. The seven factors are: (1) the intern and the employer clearly understand that there is no expectation of compensation; (2) the internship provides training similar to that given in an educational environment; (3) the internship is for the benefit of the intern; (4) the intern does not displace regular employees but works under close supervision; (5) the employer derives no immediate advantage from the intern's work; (6) the intern is not necessarily entitled to a job at the end of the internship; and (7) the employer and the intern understand that the internship is for a fixed period and the intern is not entitled to wages. If all these factors are met, an unpaid internship is likely to be considered lawful.
