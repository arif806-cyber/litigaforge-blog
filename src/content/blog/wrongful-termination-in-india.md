# Wrongful Termination in India 2026

If you've been terminated from your job without a valid reason or proper procedure, you may be a victim of wrongful termination in India. To seek justice and claim your rights, it's essential to send a legal notice to your employer, and this article will guide you through the process.

## Understanding Wrongful Termination in India

Wrongful termination, also known as unfair dismissal, occurs when an employer terminates an employee's contract without a valid reason or without following the proper procedure. In India, the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, and the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946, govern employment contracts and terminations. According to Section 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, an employer must provide a valid reason for termination and follow the procedure outlined in the employee's contract or the company's standing orders. If an employer fails to do so, the employee may file a complaint with the labor court or send a legal notice to the employer. The Supreme Court of India has also ruled in the case of Bharat Heavy Electricals Ltd. vs. S. Shiva Kumar (2004) that an employee has the right to seek relief under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, if they have been wrongfully terminated.

> **Key takeaway:** To claim relief for wrongful termination, employees must send a legal notice to their employer within the specified timeframe.

## Grounds for Sending a Legal Notice for Wrongful Termination

An employee can send a legal notice to their employer for wrongful termination on the following grounds: non-payment of wages, non-compliance with employment contracts, unfair labor practices, and violation of labor laws. The notice must specify the grounds for termination, the employee's demand for relief, and the timeline for the employer to respond. The employee must also provide evidence to support their claim, such as documents, witness statements, or records of correspondence with the employer. The labor laws in India, including the Minimum Wages Act, 1948, and the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, provide a framework for employment contracts and terminations.

> **Key takeaway:** Employees must provide sufficient evidence to support their claim for wrongful termination.

## Procedure for Sending a Legal Notice for Wrongful Termination

To send a legal notice for wrongful termination, an employee must follow these steps: 1. Determine the grounds for termination and gather evidence to support the claim. 2. Draft a legal notice specifying the grounds for termination, the employee's demand for relief, and the timeline for the employer to respond. 3. Serve the notice on the employer through registered post or email. 4. Wait for the employer's response, which must be received within the specified timeline. 5. If the employer fails to respond or disputes the claim, the employee may file a complaint with the labor court. The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, and the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, provide the framework for sending a legal notice and filing a complaint.

> **Key takeaway:** Employees must follow the proper procedure for sending a legal notice to ensure their claim is valid.

## Timeline for Sending a Legal Notice for Wrongful Termination

The timeline for sending a legal notice for wrongful termination varies depending on the jurisdiction and the grounds for termination. In India, the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, provides a timeline of 3 years from the date of termination for filing a complaint with the labor court. However, the employee must send a legal notice to the employer within a reasonable timeframe, usually 30 days from the date of termination. The employer must respond to the notice within 15 days, and if they fail to do so, the employee may file a complaint with the labor court. The Supreme Court of India has ruled in the case of Hindustan Lever Ltd. vs. Ashok Vishnu Katekar (2003) that the timeline for sending a legal notice is crucial in determining the validity of the claim.

## Penalties for Wrongful Termination in India

If an employer is found guilty of wrongful termination, they may face penalties under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, and other labor laws in India. The penalties may include reinstatement of the employee, payment of back wages, compensation for damages, and fines. The labor court may also impose penalties on the employer for non-compliance with labor laws and regulations. The Supreme Court of India has ruled in the case of Delhi Transport Corporation vs. D.T.C. Mazdoor Union (1991) that an employer must compensate an employee for wrongful termination. The penalties for wrongful termination can be severe, and employers must ensure they follow the proper procedure for termination to avoid liability.

> **Key takeaway:** Employers may face severe penalties for wrongful termination, including reinstatement and compensation.
