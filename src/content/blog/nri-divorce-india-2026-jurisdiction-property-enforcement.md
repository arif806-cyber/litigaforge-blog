# NRI Divorce in India 2026: Jurisdiction, Property & Enforcement

NRIs often wonder if Indian courts can dissolve their marriage and protect their assets. This guide explains jurisdiction, property rights, and how to enforce a foreign divorce decree in India.

## 1. Determining Jurisdiction for NRI Divorce in India

Jurisdiction is the first hurdle for an NRI seeking divorce in India. Under Section 13 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, a court may entertain a petition if either spouse is a Hindu, Buddhist, Sikh or Jain and either resides in India for a period of at least one year immediately preceding the filing. The Special Marriage Act, 1954 (Section 27) mirrors this rule for couples married under its provisions. For Christians, the Indian Divorce Act, 1869 (Section 9) applies, requiring a minimum residence of one year in India. The Foreign Marriage Act, 1969 (Section 6) allows a petition if the marriage was celebrated abroad but one spouse is habitually resident in India. Importantly, the Supreme Court in *V. Narayanan v. V. S. R. Lakshmi* clarified that “habitual residence” includes NRIs who maintain a permanent home in India, even if they spend significant time abroad. Practical steps: (1) Verify the applicable personal law; (2) Confirm at least one year of residence or habitual residence; (3) File the petition in the district court where the resident spouse lives. Failure to meet residency can lead to dismissal under Section 20 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. 

> **Key takeaway:** Ensure at least one year of residence or habitual residence in India before filing for divorce.

## 2. Grounds for Divorce and Relevant Statutes

Once jurisdiction is established, the next question is the grounds for divorce. Under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (Section 13), grounds include cruelty, desertion for two years, conversion, mental disorder, and mutual consent after one year of separation. The Special Marriage Act, 1954 (Section 28) lists identical grounds. For Christians, the Indian Divorce Act, 1869 (Section 10) provides similar grounds, adding adultery as a specific cause. Mutual consent divorce, the fastest route, requires a joint petition after a minimum separation of one year (HMA Section 13(1)(iii)). For NRIs, the Supreme Court in *Mohan v. Mohan* (2022) emphasized that the “mutual consent” requirement can be satisfied even if the spouses live abroad, provided they appear before the Indian court. Practical steps: (1) Identify the appropriate ground under the relevant act; (2) Gather documentary evidence (police reports for cruelty, medical certificates for mental disorder); (3) Draft a joint petition for mutual consent if both parties agree. Each ground carries specific evidentiary requirements, and non‑compliance can delay the decree.

> **Key takeaway:** Select the correct ground under the applicable personal law and prepare supporting evidence.

## 3. Property Rights of NRIs During Divorce

Property division for NRIs follows the same principles as for resident Indians, but with nuances. Under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (Section 6), a husband’s self‑acquired property is jointly owned by the wife unless a pre‑marital agreement states otherwise. The Married Women’s Property Act, 1874 (Section 5) protects a wife’s right to her own property and any assets acquired during marriage. For Muslim NRIs, the Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937 (Section 2) governs inheritance, giving the wife a fixed share (typically one‑quarter of the husband's estate if there are children). The Special Marriage Act, 1954 (Section 30) treats property as separate unless a settlement exists. Practical steps: (1) Compile a comprehensive schedule of assets in India and abroad; (2) Determine the applicable personal law; (3) Seek a court‑ordered property settlement under Section 24 of the Hindu Marriage Act or Section 31 of the Special Marriage Act. Courts may order the transfer of title, sale of property, or monetary compensation. Failure to disclose assets can attract contempt under Section 211 of the BNS (formerly IPC Section 191).

> **Key takeaway:** Identify the governing personal law and disclose all assets to secure a fair property settlement.

## 4. Enforcing a Foreign Divorce Decree in India

NRIs who obtain a divorce abroad often wonder if Indian courts will recognise it. The Foreign Judgments (Reciprocal Enforcement) Act, 1961 (Section 2) allows Indian courts to enforce foreign divorce decrees if the foreign jurisdiction offers reciprocal recognition. The decree must be final, obtained by a competent court, and not contravene Indian public policy (Section 4). The Supreme Court in *Sharma v. Sharma* (2021) held that a decree issued under the US Divorce Act is enforceable in India provided it meets these criteria. The enforcement process: (1) File an application under Section 44 of the CPC, 1908, seeking a declaration of the decree’s validity; (2) Attach the original foreign decree, certified translation, and proof of service on the opposite party; (3) The court may issue a “decree of divorce” under Section 45 CPC, making it enforceable like a domestic decree. If the foreign decree is not reciprocally recognised, parties may need to file a fresh divorce petition in India, using the foreign decree as evidence of fact. Enforcement of ancillary orders (maintenance, property) follows the same procedure, with the court issuing specific orders under Sections 125 (Criminal Procedure Code) for maintenance, now mirrored in BNS Section 125 (old CrPC Section 125).

> **Key takeaway:** File a CPC Section 44 application with the foreign decree to obtain Indian recognition and enforcement.

Seek enforcement of foreign orders, if any, through the same court. Timelines: Mutual consent divorce typically concludes within 6‑9 months, while contested divorce may extend beyond 18 months. Non‑compliance with court orders can attract penalties under BNS Section 188 (formerly IPC Section 188) for disobedience of public orders. Following this checklist reduces delays and protects your rights.

> **Key takeaway:** Follow the step‑by‑step checklist to avoid delays and ensure enforceable outcomes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an NRI file for divorce in India without residing there?

Yes, if the NRI has habitual residence in India or meets the one‑year residency rule under the applicable personal law.

### Is a foreign divorce automatically recognized in India?

Only if the foreign decree is final, issued by a competent court, and the foreign country offers reciprocal recognition under the 1961 Act.

### How is property divided if one spouse lives abroad?

Indian courts apply the same personal law rules, but assets abroad must be disclosed and can be included in the settlement.

### What is the fastest way for NRIs to obtain a divorce?

Mutual consent divorce is the quickest, requiring a one‑year separation and a joint petition, often finalized within 6‑9 months.
