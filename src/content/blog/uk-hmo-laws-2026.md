# UK HMO Laws 2026

The UK's Houses in Multiple Occupation (HMO) laws have undergone significant changes in recent years, impacting both landlords and tenants. ## What is an HMO?

A House in Multiple Occupation (HMO) is a property that is occupied by three or more people from more than one household, as defined in Section 254 of the Housing Act 2004. This can include shared houses, flats, and bedsits. To determine if a property is an HMO, landlords and tenants can use the UK government's online HMO checker tool. The Housing Act 2004 also outlines the requirements for HMO licensing, which includes meeting specific standards for fire safety, amenities, and space. For example, the Licensing of Houses in Multiple Occupation (Prescribed Descriptions) (England) Order 2018 requires HMOs to have a minimum of 6.51 square metres of floor space per occupant.

> **Key takeaway:** Landlords must ensure their HMO properties meet the necessary standards and are licensed to avoid penalties and fines.

## HMO Licence Rules

To obtain an HMO licence, landlords must submit an application to their local authority, providing detailed information about the property, including its location, size, and amenities. The application must also include a floor plan, a gas safety certificate, and an electrical safety certificate.
