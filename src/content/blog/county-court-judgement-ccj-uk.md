# UK CCJ 2026: Impact on Credit

## What is a County Court Judgement (CCJ)?

A County Court Judgement is a court order issued against an individual or business that fails to repay a debt, as per the County Courts Act 1984. The CCJ is usually the result of a creditor taking the debtor to court for non-payment of debt. The court will review the case and determine whether the debt is owed, and if so, order the debtor to repay the debt. The CCJ will be registered on the individual's credit file, affecting their credit score. According to the UK's Credit Reference Agencies, a CCJ can remain on an individual's credit file for six years from the date of the judgement, as stated in the Limitation Act 1980, Section 4.

## How does a CCJ affect credit?

A CCJ can significantly impact an individual's credit score, making it challenging to obtain loans, credit cards, or mortgages. Lenders view a CCJ as a high-risk indicator, as it suggests that the individual has failed to manage their debt obligations. As per the UK's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) guidelines, lenders must consider a borrower's credit history, including any CCJs, when assessing their creditworthiness. The impact of a CCJ on credit score can be mitigated by repaying the debt and obtaining a Certificate of Satisfaction, as outlined in the Civil Procedure Rules 1998, Part 40.

> **Key takeaway:** Repaying the debt and obtaining a Certificate of Satisfaction can help mitigate the impact of a CCJ on credit score.

## How to get a CCJ set aside

To get a CCJ set aside, the individual must apply to the court using a N244 form, as stated in the Civil Procedure Rules 1998, Part 23. The application must be made within 14 days of the CCJ being issued, and the individual must provide a valid reason for not responding to the original claim. The court will review the application and may grant an oral hearing, as per the UK's Civil Procedure Rules 1998, Part 39. If the CCJ is set aside, the individual can request that the creditor remove the CCJ from their credit file, as outlined in the Data Protection Act 2018, Section 46.

> **Key takeaway:** Applying to the court using a N244 form is the first step to getting a CCJ set aside.
