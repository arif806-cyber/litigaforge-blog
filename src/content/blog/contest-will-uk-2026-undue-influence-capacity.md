# How to Contest a Will in the UK 2026: Undue Influence, Lack of Capacity & Inheritance Act Claims

If you believe a recent will was forged, coerced, or ignores your legal rights, you can challenge it in court. This guide explains the three main grounds—undue influence, lack of capacity, and statutory provision claims—under UK law in 2026.

## Understanding the Legal Grounds to Contest a Will

In England and Wales, a will can be contested on three primary bases. First, **undue influence** arises when a person uses pressure, threats, or manipulation to over‑rule the testator's free will. Second, **lack of testamentary capacity** occurs when the testator cannot understand the nature of making a will, the extent of the estate, or the claims of potential beneficiaries, as defined in the Mental Capacity Act 2005, sections 2‑4. Third, a claim under the **Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975** (IPFA) can be made by anyone who was financially dependent on the deceased and believes the will does not make reasonable provision. Each ground requires different evidence, timelines, and procedural steps, but all share the need for timely legal action and clear documentation.

> **Key takeaway:** Identify which ground—undue influence, lack of capacity, or IPFA claim—best fits your situation before filing any court proceedings.

## Ground 1: Proving Undue Influence

Undue influence is not a statutory ground but a common‑law doctrine. To succeed, you must show: (1) a confidential relationship between the influencer and the testator; (2) the influencer had the opportunity to exert pressure; (3) the testator’s will deviated from prior wishes; and (4) the influencer’s conduct was the dominant cause of the change. Evidence can include emails, witness statements, medical records showing vulnerability, and any pattern of isolation. The burden of proof is on the challenger, and the standard is the balance of probabilities. If proven, the court may declare the will void or vary it to reflect the testator’s true intentions. Prompt action is essential because the limitation period for undue influence claims is six months from the grant of probate under CPR Part 36.

> **Key takeaway:** Gather contemporaneous evidence of pressure and a change in the testator’s wishes to meet the four‑part test for undue influence.

## Ground 2: Lack of Testamentary Capacity Under the Mental Capacity Act 2005

Section 2 of the Mental Capacity Act 2005 defines a person as lacking capacity if they cannot understand, retain, or weigh the information necessary to make a decision, or cannot communicate that decision. For wills, sections 2‑4 require the testator to understand: (a) the nature of making a will, (b) the extent of the property being dealt with, and (c) the claims of potential beneficiaries. Medical reports, psychiatrist assessments, and prior statements of the testator are crucial. If capacity is successfully challenged, the will is treated as if it never existed, and the estate is distributed according to an earlier valid will or intestacy rules. The six‑month limitation period from probate also applies, but a claim can be brought later if the lack of capacity is discovered after probate through a **Section 33** application to set aside the grant.

> **Key takeaway:** Secure a qualified medical assessment promptly to substantiate a lack of capacity claim before the six‑month limitation expires.

## Ground 3: Statutory Claims Under the Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975

The IPFA allows spouses, civil partners, children (including adult children), and certain dependants to claim reasonable financial provision. Section 1(1) sets out the eligible classes, while sections 2‑4 outline the factors the court considers, such as the claimant’s financial needs, the size of the estate, and the relationship with the deceased. A claim must be filed within **12 months** of the grant of probate (or within 12 months of the date of death if the grant is delayed). The court can order a variation of the will, a financial award, or a lump‑sum payment. In 2026, recent amendments to the IPFA (effective 1 January 2026) introduced a higher threshold for “reasonable provision” for estates exceeding £5 million, requiring the court to weigh public interest and tax implications more heavily.

> **Key takeaway:** File an IPFA claim within 12 months of probate to secure a statutory right to reasonable provision.

## Practical Steps to Contest a Will in 2026

1. **Preserve Evidence** – Secure the original will, any drafts, correspondence, and medical records. 2. **Seek Legal Advice** – A solicitor experienced in probate and family law will assess the strongest ground and advise on deadlines. 3. **Obtain a Medical Report** – For capacity challenges, arrange an assessment by a consultant psychiatrist. 4. **Draft a Claim** – Use the appropriate court form: **Form PA1A** for IPFA claims, **Form PA2** for contesting a will on any ground. 5. **File Within the Limitation Period** – Six months for undue influence or lack of capacity; 12 months for IPFA. 6. **Prepare for Mediation** – Courts encourage early settlement; be ready with a clear statement of loss and proposed remedy. 7. **Attend Court** – If mediation fails, the judge will consider the evidence and may order a variation, a new grant of probate, or distribution under intestacy rules. 8. **Consider Cross‑Border Issues** – If assets are located in India, UAE or other jurisdictions, coordinate with local counsel to enforce the UK judgment abroad. 9. **Cost Management** – Apply for a **Costs Budget** under CPR Part 46 to control legal fees; the court may award costs against the losing party. 10. **Post‑Judgment Enforcement** – Use the **UK Enforcement Act 2014** to seize assets if the respondent refuses to comply.

By following these steps, claimants can navigate the complex probate landscape efficiently and increase their chances of a favorable outcome.

> **Key takeaway:** Act quickly, preserve evidence, and engage a specialist solicitor to meet strict filing deadlines.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the deadline to contest a will in the UK?

You must file a claim within six months of the grant of probate for undue influence or lack of capacity, and within 12 months for an IPFA claim.

### Can I contest a will if I am not a family member?

Only those with a legal interest—spouse, civil partner, child, or dependant—can bring an IPFA claim; others must rely on undue influence or lack of capacity grounds.

### Do I need a lawyer to challenge a will?

While you can represent yourself, a solicitor experienced in probate greatly improves your chances of meeting deadlines and presenting persuasive evidence.

### How does the Mental Capacity Act affect my claim?

The Act sets the legal test for capacity; a qualified medical report confirming the testator lacked capacity is essential to succeed.
