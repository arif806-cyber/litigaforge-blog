# Renters Reform Bill UK 2026: Essential Tenant Protections

The 2026 Renters Reform Bill reshapes the landlord‑tenant landscape across England and Wales, giving renters stronger security and clearer rights. Here's what every tenant must know to protect themselves under the new law.

## 1. End of Fixed‑Term Tenancies and Automatic Periodic Tenancy

The Bill abolishes the automatic conversion of fixed‑term tenancies into periodic tenancies under the Housing Act 1988 s.21(1). Instead, landlords must serve a new "Section 21‑style" notice that complies with the statutory form introduced by the Bill (Schedule 2, s.2). Tenants now enjoy a minimum 6‑month notice period before a landlord can seek possession, up from the previous 2‑month requirement. This change reduces sudden evictions and gives renters ample time to find alternative accommodation. Practical steps: (i) check your tenancy agreement for the new notice clause; (ii) keep records of any notice received; (iii) if notice is less than six months, raise a formal objection within 14 days using the prescribed form.

Key takeaway: Tenants now have a guaranteed six‑month notice period before a landlord can end a tenancy, strengthening housing stability.

> **Key takeaway:** Always verify that any eviction notice provides at least six months' notice as required by the 2026 Bill.

## 2. Rent Increase Caps and Transparency

Under the new provisions (Housing Act 1988 amendment s.13A), landlords may only increase rent once per year and must give a minimum of two months' written notice. The increase cannot exceed the median rent for comparable properties in the local area, as published by the Office for National Statistics (ONS) and referenced in Schedule 3, s.5 of the Bill. Failure to comply can result in a civil penalty of up to £5,000 per breach under the Housing (Regulation) Act 2025 s.45. Tenants should: (i) request the ONS median rent data; (ii) compare the proposed increase; (iii) challenge excessive hikes through a written objection within 14 days, citing the statutory cap.

Key takeaway: Rent hikes are now capped to local median rates and require a two‑month notice, giving tenants a clear benchmark to contest unfair increases.

> **Key takeaway:** Compare any proposed rent increase to the ONS median and object within 14 days if it exceeds the statutory cap.

## 3. Security Deposit Protection and Return

The Bill expands the scope of the Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS) by mandating that all deposits, regardless of tenancy type, be placed in a government‑approved protected scheme within 30 days of receipt (Housing Act 2004 s.213(1) as amended by the 2026 Bill). Additionally, landlords must provide an itemised deduction statement within 14 days of tenancy end, and any disputed amount must be resolved through the TDS dispute service within 30 days. Non‑compliance attracts a fine of up to £10,000 per violation under the Housing (Regulation) Act 2025 s.48. Tenants should: (i) obtain the scheme certificate; (ii) keep a copy of the deposit receipt; (iii) promptly raise disputes through the scheme’s online portal.

Key takeaway: Deposits are now universally protected, and landlords must return them promptly with clear itemisation, or face hefty fines.

> **Key takeaway:** Ensure your deposit is protected by a TDS scheme and demand an itemised statement within 14 days of moving out.

## 4. Right to a “Rent Repayment Order” for Illegal Evictions

The Renters Reform Bill introduces a streamlined Rent Repayment Order (RRO) process under the Housing Act 2004 s.81A (new). Tenants who experience an illegal eviction—such as a notice that does not meet the six‑month requirement—can apply for an RRO within 12 months of the eviction. The order can compel the landlord to repay up to 12 months' rent, plus interest, and may also impose a civil penalty of up to £7,500 under the Housing (Regulation) Act 2025 s.50. To claim, tenants must: (i) complete the RRO application form (Form RRO‑2026); (ii) attach the illegal notice and tenancy agreement; (iii) submit within the 12‑month window. Legal aid may cover fees if the tenant’s income is below the statutory threshold.

Key takeaway: Tenants can recover up to a year's rent if evicted illegally, provided they act within a year of the breach.

> **Key takeaway:** File an RRO within 12 months of an illegal eviction to reclaim lost rent and potentially secure a penalty against the landlord.
